# Tokenbound research: working notes

Consolidation of `Planv1/research/tokenbound/` (three reports and six notes files) against the Planv2 decision register. Citations use the form `file.md > Section heading`; notes files are cited as `notes/<file>.md > Section`. The labels **Verified** and **Inferred** are the research's own labels. **Inference** marks reasoning that is this note's own and is not stated in the sources.

## 1. Coverage and verdict

The research examined three repositories at pinned commits: `tokenbound/contracts` at `bce75f9` (tag `v0.3.1` plus 15 commits, last commit 2024-03-11, MIT SPDX headers), `erc6551/reference` at `da16a63` (`v0.3.0` plus 17 commits, 2023-10-24, MIT), and `tokenbound/sdk` at `6244f1e` (`@tokenbound/sdk` 0.5.5, 2024-10-22, ISC per package.json, no LICENSE file), plus the submodules OpenZeppelin v4.9.3, account-abstraction v0.6.0 (EntryPoint v0.6, GPL-3.0) and multicall-authenticated `653b2bc` (`01-codebase-report.md > 1.1 Repositories`; `notes/00-phase0-orientation.md > Repositories and versions`). It read `AccountV3`, `AccountV3Upgradable`, `AccountProxy`, `AccountGuardian`, every abstract (Lockable, Overridable, Permissioned, Signatory, ERC4337Account, ERC6551Account, the executors and sandbox), the registry, the cross-chain executors, the repo tests, the Zellic and CertiK audit PDFs, and the SDK's `TokenboundClient` (`01-codebase-report.md > 3.1 Contracts`; `notes/A-account-core.md` header; `notes/C-sdk.md > 1. Public API`). It also made read-only JSON-RPC calls against Monad mainnet (143), Monad testnet (10143) and Ethereum to confirm what is deployed and to compare runtime code hashes (`01-codebase-report.md > 1.2 What is deployed on Monad`; `notes/B-registry-deploy-trust.md > 6. Deterministic deployment and verification`). Nothing was compiled or executed because Foundry was not installed, so every behavioral claim marked Inferred is still waiting on the spike plan (`01-codebase-report.md` header; `03-spike-plan.md` header). The overall verdict: use the canonical Tokenbound v3 deployment on Monad unmodified, but only as the agent's skill inventory and identity anchor, never as a capital account; grant no permissions on it, set no overrides on it, keep capital in our own `PersonalAccount` and `StrategyVault`, and protect sales by escrow custody rather than by Tokenbound's `lock()` and `state()`. This is "Option A" in `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.5 Use unmodified, extend, or replace`, with the reasoning in `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.1 Role decision`. A custom ERC-6551 implementation (Option C) is named as a later possibility, not a launch requirement.

## 2. Facts the final plan must carry

### 2.1 Account implementation: ownership and nesting

- A TBA is an ERC-1167 clone whose bytecode footer holds `(salt, chainId, tokenContract, tokenId)`; `token()` reads it with `extcodecopy`, so the binding can never change (**Verified**). `01-codebase-report.md > 4.1 Ownership`; `notes/A-account-core.md > 1. Owner resolution`.
- `owner()` calls `IERC721.ownerOf(tokenId)` live on every authorization check, inside a try/catch. There is no cached owner and the account is never notified of a transfer. It returns `address(0)` if the token's chainId differs from `block.chainid`, if the token contract has no code, or if `ownerOf` reverts (**Verified**). `01-codebase-report.md > 4.1 Ownership`; `notes/A-account-core.md > 1. Owner resolution`.
- `_rootTokenOwner` walks up while the owner is itself an ERC-6551 account, with no depth cap. The walk only recognises clones whose embedded implementation equals `__self`, the logic contract `0x41C8...`. Canonical TBAs are clones of the `AccountProxy` `0x5526...`, so for canonical accounts the walk almost certainly stops at the direct owner and `executeNested` computes wrong addresses. The repo only tests nesting with the bare implementation (**Inferred**, high confidence; spike 9a). `01-codebase-report.md > 4.1 Ownership`; `notes/A-account-core.md > 1. Owner resolution`; `notes/B-registry-deploy-trust.md > 1. Registry` (side finding); `notes/D-security.md > 1.7`.
- Consequence if confirmed: a nested canonical TBA's permissions and overrides are keyed to the parent TBA address, which does not change when the top-level NFT is sold, so they would survive a sale (**Inferred**). `notes/A-account-core.md > 6. CRITICAL: permissions and overrides across NFT transfer` (Nested accounts).
- Cross-chain: for a token on another chain, `owner()` is zero and only the EntryPoint (needing a signature that cannot exist), the OP-stack L1 alias, or a guardian-trusted executor can act. None of these are active for Monad-native TBAs (**Verified** code, **Inferred** for our case). `01-codebase-report.md > 4.1 Ownership`; `notes/A-account-core.md > 1. Owner resolution` (Cross-chain).

### 2.2 Account implementation: execution

- `_isValidExecutor(executor)` returns true in this order: the EntryPoint; (foreign-chain accounts only) the OP alias or a guardian-trusted executor; the direct owner; the root owner; any address in `permissions[rootOwner][executor]` (**Verified**). `01-codebase-report.md > 4.2 Execution entry points`; `notes/A-account-core.md > 2. Execution entry points`.
- The caller is `_msgSender()` (ERC-2771). The authenticated Multicall3 forwarder `0xcA11...CCD` appends its own `msg.sender` to every sub-call, so it cannot be used to spoof a caller. `lock`, `setPermissions` and `setOverrides` use raw `msg.sender` and therefore cannot be batched through the forwarder or called through the EntryPoint (**Verified**). `notes/A-account-core.md > 2. Execution entry points`; `notes/B-registry-deploy-trust.md > 6. Deterministic deployment and verification` (Multicall forwarder).
- `execute(to, value, data, op)` supports op 0 CALL, 1 DELEGATECALL, 2 CREATE, 3 CREATE2. DELEGATECALL never runs in the account's storage: the account CALLs a per-account sandbox (CREATE2 by the account with salt `keccak256("org.tokenbound.sandbox")`) which delegatecalls the target. The sandbox may only be called by its account, and it can call back into `extcall`, `extcreate`, `extcreate2` and `extsload` (**Verified**, sandbox bytecode decoded). `01-codebase-report.md > 4.2 Execution entry points`; `notes/A-account-core.md > 2. Execution entry points`; `notes/D-security.md > 4.2 Sandbox bytecode`.
- `executeBatch` does one authorization check and one lock/state update for the whole batch and reverts wholesale on any failure. `executeNested` walks an ownership proof, checks the lock of deployed intermediates, but does not bump intermediate state (**Verified**). `notes/A-account-core.md > 2. Execution entry points`; `notes/D-security.md > 1.6 Nested accounts`.
- Lock and `state()` coverage by entry point (**Verified**): `execute*`, `lock`, `setPermissions`, `setOverrides` check the lock and bump state. `extcall`/`extcreate*`, `upgradeTo*`, `receive`/`fallback`/token hooks (override path), ERC-4337 `validateUserOp` and prefund do neither. `01-codebase-report.md > 4.2 Execution entry points` (table); `notes/D-security.md > 0.2 Path-by-path coverage of lock and state`.
- No reentrancy guard exists anywhere in `contracts/src`; state is updated before the external call, and only authorized executors can re-enter. Guards for our flows must live in our Executor (**Verified** absence; risk **Inferred** low). `01-codebase-report.md > 7. Security and edge cases` (S14); `notes/D-security.md > 4.3 Reentrancy guards`.

### 2.3 Account implementation: signatures (ERC-1271 and isValidSigner)

- `isValidSigner(signer, data)` ignores `data` and returns the magic value for the direct owner, the root owner, or any permissioned address (**Verified**). `notes/A-account-core.md > 3. Signatures`.
- `isValidSignature(hash, sig)` accepts (a) a 65-byte ECDSA signature from a valid signer, or (b) a `v == 0` contract signature with the signer address in `r` and the inner-signature offset in `s`, verified through ERC-1271 on that signer, which must itself be a valid signer or the account (**Verified**). `01-codebase-report.md > 4.3 Signatures (ERC-1271)`; `notes/A-account-core.md > 3. Signatures`.
- The raw hash is checked with no account address, chainId or EIP-712 wrapping. A signature accepted by one TBA is accepted by every TBA with the same owner, and by the owner EOA, unless the signed payload names the account (EIP-3009 `from`, EIP-2612 `owner`, Seaport `offerer` do; Permit2's typehashes do not) (**Verified** mechanism, replay **Inferred**). `01-codebase-report.md > 4.3 Signatures (ERC-1271)`; `notes/D-security.md > 5.1 No domain wrapping`.
- The lock does not affect signatures: there is no `isLocked` check in the signature path. The repo test that suggests otherwise signs with a non-owner key (**Verified**). `01-codebase-report.md > 4.3 Signatures (ERC-1271)`; `notes/A-account-core.md > 3. Signatures`.
- Signatures shorter than 65 bytes revert instead of returning `0xffffffff` because `signature[64]` is read unconditionally (**Verified**). `01-codebase-report.md > 4.3 Signatures (ERC-1271)`.
- After a transfer, verification resolves to the new owner, so the seller's signatures fail for anything verified afterwards. Effects already recorded onchain (a Permit2 or USDC allowance, an executed order) are not undone (**Verified** live check; persistence **Inferred**). `01-codebase-report.md > 4.3 Signatures (ERC-1271)`; `notes/D-security.md > 5.1 No domain wrapping`.
- ERC-4337 UserOps are bound: EntryPoint v0.6's `userOpHash` includes the EntryPoint address, chainId, `sender` and `nonce`, and the account signs `toEthSignedMessageHash(userOpHash)` (**Verified**). `notes/D-security.md > 5.1 No domain wrapping`.

### 2.4 Account implementation: state, permissions, lock, overrides (the "extension" mechanisms)

- `state()` is `keccak256(abi.encode(_state, _msgData()))`, updated only on `execute*`, `lock`, `setPermissions`, `setOverrides`. It does not change on NFT transfer, incoming tokens, `upgradeTo*`, override-driven calls, or ERC-4337 prefund (**Verified**). It only covers the account's own execute and config paths (**Inferred**). `01-codebase-report.md > 4.4 State`; `notes/A-account-core.md > 4. state()`; `notes/D-security.md > 0.1`.
- Permissions: `permissions[rootOwner][caller] = bool`, set only by the root owner with a direct `msg.sender`, only while unlocked. It is all-or-nothing: a permissioned address can `execute` anything with any value and any op, batch, upgrade to a trusted implementation, and produce ERC-1271 and ERC-4337 signatures. It cannot call `lock`, `setPermissions` or `setOverrides`. There is no target, selector, amount or expiry scoping (**Verified**). `01-codebase-report.md > 4.5 Permissions`; `notes/A-account-core.md > 5. Permissions`.
- After a transfer, lookups use `permissions[newOwner]`, which is empty, so every grant stops in the same block. Nothing deletes the old entries: if the NFT returns to a previous owner, that owner's old grants revive (**Verified** storage; risk **Inferred**). `01-codebase-report.md > 4.5 Permissions`; `notes/A-account-core.md > 6. CRITICAL: permissions and overrides across NFT transfer`; `notes/D-security.md > 7. Upgrade, override and permission persistence`.
- Lock: `lock(untilTimestamp)` only by the root owner directly; max `block.timestamp + 365 days`; cannot be extended, shortened or cancelled until expiry; `lockedUntil` is a single slot not keyed by owner, so a seller's lock binds the buyer. It blocks `execute*`, `setPermissions`, `setOverrides`, `lock`. It does not block ERC-1271 signatures, ERC-4337 validation and prefund, `upgradeTo`, or overrides (**Verified**). While locked the owner cannot revoke permissions (**Verified**). `01-codebase-report.md > 4.6 Lock`; `notes/A-account-core.md > 7. Lock`.
- Overrides: `overrides[rootOwner][selector] = implementation`, set only by the root owner while unlocked. They apply only to `receive`, `fallback` (unknown selectors), the ERC-721 and ERC-1155 receive hooks, and `supportsInterface` for non-base IDs; built-in functions cannot be overridden. A mutating override runs in the sandbox with the original caller appended and can move account assets through `extcall` with no lock check and no state bump (**Verified** path; drain **Inferred**). Overrides go dormant on transfer and revive on return, like permissions. The repo test for the reset checks the wrong account (**Verified** test weakness). `01-codebase-report.md > 4.7 Overrides`; `notes/A-account-core.md > 8. Overrides`; `notes/D-security.md > 1.3`.
- Receive hooks: all incoming ETH, ERC-20, ERC-721 and ERC-1155 are accepted by default; ERC-20 and plain ERC-721 `transferFrom` cannot be refused at all. The only rejection is `safeTransferFrom` of the account's own bound token (`OwnershipCycle`), checked before the override. Plain `transferFrom` of the own token, or two accounts owning each other, bricks the accounts (**Verified** check; bypasses **Inferred**). `01-codebase-report.md > 7. Security and edge cases` (S8, S9); `notes/D-security.md > 2. Ownership cycles`; `notes/D-security.md > 3. Unsolicited tokens`.

### 2.5 Registry and deterministic addresses

- The registry `0x000000006551c19487814612e58FE06813775758` deploys an ERC-1167 clone by CREATE2 with `abi.encode(salt, chainId, tokenContract, tokenId)` appended. The address depends only on (registry, implementation, salt, chainId, tokenContract, tokenId); `chainId` is a parameter, not `block.chainid`. Init code is 183 bytes, runtime 173 bytes (`0xAD`) (**Verified**). `01-codebase-report.md > 5.1 Registry`; `notes/B-registry-deploy-trust.md > 1. Registry`.
- `createAccount` has no access control, is idempotent, and returns the existing address without an event or revert if already deployed. `account(...)` computes the address without deploying (**Verified**). Same sources.
- The formula was confirmed against the live Monad registry by `eth_call` and a Python recomputation (**Verified**). `notes/B-registry-deploy-trust.md > 1. Registry`.
- For Tokenbound v3 the "implementation" argument is the `AccountProxy` `0x55266d75D1a14E4572138116aF39863Ed6596E7F`, not the logic contract `0x41C8f39463A868d3A88af00cd0fe7102F30E44eC`. Runtime call chain: clone delegatecalls AccountProxy, which delegatecalls the implementation stored in the clone's own ERC-1967 slot (**Verified**). `01-codebase-report.md > 5.1 Registry`; `notes/B-registry-deploy-trust.md > 1. Registry`; `notes/C-sdk.md > 2. Mechanisms` (Address computation).
- Choosing a different implementation argument yields a different TBA address (**Verified**). `notes/B-registry-deploy-trust.md > 2. Upgradeability`.

### 2.6 Deployment and trust

- Canonical addresses with code on both Monad mainnet and testnet, with runtime keccak equal to Ethereum (**Verified**): Registry `0x0000...5758` (571 bytes), AccountProxy `0x5526...6E7F` (902), AccountV3Upgradable `0x41C8...44eC` (14924), AccountGuardian `0x2FE5ccb0d7Ea195FEb87987d3573F9fcCE2b5D57` (1211), Multicall3 forwarder `0xcA1167915584462449EE5b4Ea51c37fE81eCDCCD` (4126), EntryPoint v0.6 `0x5FF137D4b0FDCD49DcA30c7CF57E578a026d2789` (23689), CREATE2 deployer `0x4e59b44847b379578588920cA78FbF26c0B4956C`. `01-codebase-report.md > 1.2 What is deployed on Monad`; `notes/B-registry-deploy-trust.md > 6. Deterministic deployment and verification` (a).
- Deployment order and salts: Guardian(tokenboundSafe), then AccountV3Upgradable(entryPoint, forwarder, registry, guardian), then AccountProxy(guardian, implementation), all with salt `0x6551...6551` through the 0x4e59 factory; the registry is deployed separately with its own salt. CREATE2 recomputation from Ethereum creation transactions matches all five addresses; constructor args match the script; compiler metadata says solc 0.8.17. Because Monad has the same factory, addresses and runtime hashes, the Monad contracts were built from the same init code (**Verified** recomputation; Monad conclusion **Inferred**, strong). Compiling `contracts@v0.3.1` to reproduce the init code is the remaining step (spike 9d). `01-codebase-report.md > 3.1 Contracts`; `notes/B-registry-deploy-trust.md > 6. Deterministic deployment and verification` (b).
- The only source difference between tag v0.3.1 and HEAD is `virtual` keywords on six functions and import paths; logic is the same (**Verified** diff, **Inferred** equivalence). `01-codebase-report.md > 1.1 Repositories`; `notes/00-phase0-orientation.md > Source diff between v0.3.1 tag and HEAD`.
- Guardian: `Ownable2Step` with `isTrustedImplementation` and `isTrustedExecutor` allowlists; immutable in both the implementation and the proxy; no account can opt out (**Verified**). `01-codebase-report.md > 5.3 Guardian`; `notes/B-registry-deploy-trust.md > 3. Guardian`.
- Guardian owner on Monad mainnet is the Tokenbound Safe `0x781b6A527482828bB04F33563797d4b696ddF328`, a 3-of-4 Safe v1.3.0 with the same signers as Ethereum, nonce 0, no modules. So no guardian setting has ever been changed on Monad and all trust lists are at defaults (**Verified** state; conclusion **Inferred**, high confidence). On Monad testnet the Safe address has no code, so the testnet guardian is frozen at defaults (**Verified** absence). `01-codebase-report.md > 1.2 What is deployed on Monad`; `notes/B-registry-deploy-trust.md > 3. Guardian` (Guardian onchain).
- `isTrustedImplementation` is false for both `0x41C8` and `0x5526` on Monad, so no `upgradeTo` can succeed there today and every initialized canonical TBA must point at `0x41C8` (**Verified** reads; conclusion **Inferred**). Same sources.
- On Ethereum the guardian trusts the LayerZero executor `0x0F22...` (event at block 19434674). A trusted executor has full power only over accounts whose token lives on a different chain. Our TBA addresses exist counterfactually on every chain with the canonical registry; on those chains they are foreign accounts with `owner() == 0`, controlled by Tokenbound's trusted executors and not by the NFT holder (**Verified** mechanism; exposure **Inferred**). `01-codebase-report.md > 5.3 Guardian`; `notes/B-registry-deploy-trust.md > 3. Guardian`; `notes/B-registry-deploy-trust.md > Trust assumptions table` (T2).
- `AccountProxy.initialize(impl)` has no caller check, runs once, and accepts only the initial implementation or a guardian-trusted one. Until it runs, the proxy delegates to `address(0)`: plain MON and ERC-20 transfers in succeed, but `safeTransferFrom` of ERC-721 or ERC-1155 reverts because the hook returns empty data (**Verified** code; consequence **Inferred**). `01-codebase-report.md > 5.2 Upgradeability`; `notes/B-registry-deploy-trust.md > 2. Upgradeability`.
- `upgradeTo` requires `guardian.isTrustedImplementation(impl)` and `_isValidExecutor(_msgSender())`, so the owner, any permissioned address, or a UserOp can upgrade. An upgrade survives NFT transfer, is not blocked by the lock, and does not bump state (**Verified**). `01-codebase-report.md > 5.2 Upgradeability`; `notes/B-registry-deploy-trust.md > 2. Upgradeability`.
- Any contract can read a TBA's ERC-1967 slot through the public view `extsload(bytes32)`, so an escrow can check which implementation a TBA runs (**Verified**). `01-codebase-report.md > 5.2 Upgradeability`; `notes/D-security.md > 7. Upgrade, override and permission persistence`. Note: `notes/B-registry-deploy-trust.md > 2. Upgradeability` states the opposite ("a sale/escrow contract cannot read another contract's storage slot directly"); the final report corrects this and spike 9b confirms it. See section 6.

UserOps are signed EIP-191 over `userOpHash` by the owner, root owner or a permissioned address; nonces are the EntryPoint's; standard paymasters should work. EntryPoint v0.7 and v0.8 are also deployed on Monad but the TBA does not accept them. - Is Monad "supported" per the research: neither repo mentions Monad; the SDK's `chainIdToChain` throws for 143 and 10143; but every canonical contract is deployed on Monad with identical code, and the SDK works if a viem `chain` object is passed (**Verified**). `01-codebase-report.md > 1.1 Repositories`; `01-codebase-report.md > 6. ### 2.7 SDK capabilities and limitations

- `TokenboundClient` is the only supported V3 entry point. The standalone functions exported from the package root (`getAccount`, `createAccount`, `prepareExecuteCall`, ...) are deprecated V2 versions defaulting to the V2 registry (**Verified**). `01-codebase-report.md > 3.2 SDK`; `notes/C-sdk.md > 1a`.
- The V3 address constants (`ERC_6551_DEFAULT`, forwarder address) are not exported from the root; we must copy them (**Verified**). `notes/C-sdk.md > 1a`.
- Constructor: `chainId: 143` alone throws (`chainIdToChain` has a fixed list); pass a viem `chain`. viem `2.21.32` (lockfile) has no `monad` chain; viem 2.53.1 and later do; `defineChain` works otherwise (**Verified** for local installs; boundary **Inferred**). `notes/C-sdk.md > 1b`; `notes/C-sdk.md > 3. Chain configuration and Monad`.
- `getAccount` computes the address offline. `createAccount` sends one `aggregate3` through the forwarder: `registry.createAccount(AccountProxy, ...)` then `AccountProxy.initialize(0x41C8...)` then optional `appendedCalls`; it does not wait for a receipt. A second call for an initialized account reverts (`AlreadyInitialized`, `allowFailure: false`), so creation is not idempotent (**Verified** mechanism; revert **Inferred**). With a custom `implementationAddress` the SDK does no initialization and rejects `appendedCalls` (**Verified**). `notes/C-sdk.md > 2. Mechanisms` (Account creation).
- `execute` always uses op 0 CALL (the `operation` parameter is ignored); a `chainId` different from the client's silently switches to the LayerZero path, which has no Monad entry (**Verified**). `notes/C-sdk.md > 2. Mechanisms` (Execution).
- Transfer helpers take amounts as JS numbers (precision risk, decimals above 18 rejected); recipients may be `.eth` names resolved via ENS, which fails on Monad (**Verified** code; failure **Inferred**). Same section.
- `signMessage` signs with the connected EOA; it does not produce a TBA ERC-1271 signature. `isValidSigner` checks only the connected address (**Verified**). `notes/C-sdk.md > 2. Mechanisms` (Signing and validity).
- `deconstructBytecode` reports `AccountProxy` as the implementation; the SDK has no method to read the ERC-1967 slot (**Verified**). Same section.
- Not in the SDK: `setPermissions`, `lock`, `setOverrides`, `executeBatch`, `executeNested`, `upgradeTo`, `state`, `isValidSignature`, ERC-4337, the guardian. A thin viem wrapper over the exported `erc6551AccountAbiV3` is needed (**Verified**). `01-codebase-report.md > 6. SDK public API and Monad setup`; `notes/C-sdk.md > 4. Contract features vs SDK coverage`.
- The V3 ABIs were generated from Etherscan mainnet; the README's "encode call" example uses a deprecated function that throws under V3; the ethers `signMessage` type guard is a no-op; last commit 2024-10-22, no Monad awareness (**Verified**). `notes/C-sdk.md > 5. Caveats and version-specific behavior` (#12, #16, #18, #21).
- SDK tests run Vitest against an `@viem/anvil` fork of Ethereum mainnet with Zora fixtures; the same harness can fork Monad with our own test tokens, ENS tests dropped and the fork block pinned (**Verified** pattern; reuse **Inferred**). `notes/C-sdk.md > 6. How tests run`.

### 2.8 Security findings (severity is for our platform)

Numbered S1 to S15 in `01-codebase-report.md > 7. Security and edge cases` and F1 to F15 in `notes/D-security.md > Findings table`; the two lists align one to one.

- S1/F1 Override drain bypasses lock and state (High). `notes/D-security.md > 1.3`.
- S2/F2 ERC-1271 ignores the lock; an owner or permissioned signer can sign Permit2, EIP-2612 or EIP-3009 payloads during a lock; Monad USDC has the bytes-signature entry points (High). `notes/D-security.md > 1.4`.
- S3/F3 Approvals granted earlier outlive lock and transfer; nothing in the account tracks or revokes them (High). `notes/D-security.md > 1.5`; `notes/D-security.md > 6. Approvals left behind`.
- S4/F4 Permissions are all-or-nothing and include upgrade and signing (High if a session key is ever permissioned; none if only a restricted contract is). `01-codebase-report.md > 7`.
- S5/F5 Grants revive when the NFT returns to a previous owner (Medium). `notes/D-security.md > 7`.
- S6/F6 The lock carries over to the buyer, up to 365 days (Medium). `notes/A-account-core.md > 7. Lock`.
- S7/F7 Upgrades are not locked and do not bump state; limited to guardian-trusted implementations, none on Monad today (Medium, trust in Tokenbound). `notes/B-registry-deploy-trust.md > 2. Upgradeability`.
- S8/F8 Ownership cycles brick accounts; only safe self-transfer is blocked (Medium; AgentNFT must block). `notes/D-security.md > 2. Ownership cycles`.
- S9/F9 Unsolicited tokens cannot be refused (Medium; builds must never derive from balances). `notes/D-security.md > 3. Unsolicited tokens`.
- S10/F10 No domain separation in ERC-1271; cross-account replay for payloads that do not name the account (Medium). `notes/D-security.md > 5.1`.
- S11/F11 Nested root owner inert for canonical accounts (Low; do not nest). `notes/D-security.md > 1.7`.
- S12/F13 Uninitialized proxy window; `initialize` is permissionless (Low if atomic). `notes/D-security.md > 7`.
- S13/F14 ERC-4337 prefund is paid during a lock; a signer acting as bundler could leak MON (Low). `notes/A-account-core.md > 7. Lock`.
- S14 No reentrancy guard (Low for the account). `notes/D-security.md > 4.3`.
- S15 Sandboxed delegatecall cannot write account storage (Positive). `notes/D-security.md > 4.2`.

## 3. Platform mapping: what the research recommends we build

### 3.1 Role of the TBA

- At launch the TBA is the agent's skill inventory and identity anchor: it holds equipped SkillNFTs, is the stable address bound to the AgentNFT and the ERC-8004 identity, and optionally signs ERC-1271 identity messages with the owner as signer. It does not hold trading capital, vault assets, or operating funds. `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.1 Role decision`.
- Five reasons: permissions cannot be restricted except by pointing at our own contract, at which point the TBA adds nothing; capital in the TBA moves with the NFT; four channels (overrides, ERC-1271, approvals, upgrades) move assets around lock and state; guardian trust would then cover user capital; the TBA speaks only EntryPoint v0.6. `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.1 Role decision` (Why).
- The TBA could hold personal capital only under conditions C1 to C6 (Executor as the only permissioned address; Executor never forwards raw calldata, never calls `upgradeTo`, uses only op 0, does not implement `isValidSignature`; Executor checks an ownership epoch; exact approvals cleared in the same transaction, no Permit2; sales through our escrow with sweep, no overrides, no lock, expected implementation; accept guardian trust). The research concludes a separate PersonalAccount is lower risk. `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.1 Role decision` (Can the TBA safely hold...); `notes/A-account-core.md > Implications for our platform` (2).

### 3.2 AgentNFT and the TBA

- AgentNFT mint calls `registry.createAccount(AccountProxy, salt 0, 143, AgentNFT, id)` and `AccountProxy.initialize(0x41C8...)` in the same transaction, which prevents an `initialize` front-run and the uninitialized window in which ERC-1155 transfers revert. A test asserts the implementation slot after mint. `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.2 Component mapping`; `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.6 Risks, ranked` (12).
- AgentNFT `_update` bumps `ownerEpoch[agentId]` on every transfer, including into and out of escrow. `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.3 Transfer design` (steps 4, 8, 9).
- AgentNFT rejects transfers to any agent TBA and to the token's own TBA (closes S8), and must not allow burn while the TBA holds anything. `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.4 Recommended contract design` (Components); `notes/D-security.md > 2. Ownership cycles`.
- AgentNFT must be a standard ERC-721 whose `ownerOf` cannot be changed by surprise, because `ownerOf` is the whole control model (CertiK EXA-01). `notes/D-security.md > 8.2 CertiK`.
- Interface sketch (illustrative): `IAgentNFT.ownerEpoch(agentId) -> uint64`, `tbaOf(agentId) -> address`, `tier(agentId) -> uint8`. `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.4 Recommended contract design` (Interface sketches).
- History is indexed by `agentId`, unchanged by transfer; BuildRegistry keeps an epoch log. `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.2 Component mapping` (Transfer of history).

### 3.3 Skills: how they are held and equipped

- Equipping means the owner transfers a SkillNFT (ERC-1155) into the TBA; the TBA's ERC1155Holder hooks accept it. Unequipping is the owner calling `TBA.execute` to transfer it out. `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.2 Component mapping` (Skill inventory, Owner withdrawals).
- SkillNFT refuses `safeTransferFrom` out of an agent TBA unless `msg.sender == from` (no operator pulls) and refuses `setApprovalForAll` when the owner is an agent TBA. It recognises TBAs through `registry.account(...)` or a mapping set by AgentNFT. `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.4 Recommended contract design` (Components); `notes/D-security.md > 6. Approvals left behind`.
- BuildRegistry stores `(agentId, epoch, buildHash, skills[], versions[])`, set only by the current owner, checks slots by tier and that the TBA holds each listed skill at activation, and bumps `configEpoch`. Slots are enforced here, never by counting balances, because anyone can push tokens into the TBA. `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.2 Component mapping` (Skill slot enforcement, Active build separation); `notes/D-security.md > 3. Unsolicited tokens`.
- Interface sketch: `IBuildRegistry.setBuild(agentId, skillIds[], versions[])` (owner only), `buildHash(agentId) -> bytes32`, `configEpoch(agentId) -> uint64`. `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.4 Recommended contract design` (Interface sketches).
- Indexers and BuildRegistry recognise only our SkillNFT contract; a malicious token can emit Transfer events naming the TBA, so indexers read our registry, not events. Do not set overrides to reject spam. `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.2 Component mapping` (Unsolicited token handling); `notes/D-security.md > 3. Unsolicited tokens`.

### 3.4 What "skill-drain path" means and how the research closes it

- A skill-drain path is any way the seller can move skills (or anything else) out of the TBA between listing and settlement without the buyer detecting it. The research identifies four: (1) plain `execute`, detectable via `state()` and blockable by `lock()`; (2) a pre-installed override on `fallback`, `receive` or a token hook that calls `extcall`, which bypasses both lock and state; (3) ERC-1271 signatures for Permit2, EIP-2612 or EIP-3009, which bypass both; (4) an approval or `setApprovalForAll` granted before listing, which lets a spender pull at any time. `notes/D-security.md > 1. Transfer (front-run) attacks against a sale`.
- Closure: while listed, the AgentNFT sits in our `AgentEscrow`. The TBA's root owner is then the escrow, so the seller's permissions and overrides are keyed to the wrong address and go dormant, and the seller can no longer execute or sign (`_isValidSigner` resolves to the escrow, which has no `isValidSignature`). That closes (2) and (3) during the listing. (4) is closed by SkillNFT refusing operator transfers out of agent TBAs. S6 and S7 are closed by listing-time checks. `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.3 Transfer design` (Key idea).
- Tokenbound's own `lock()` plus `state()` is marked **Conflict** (not sufficient on its own). `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.2 Component mapping` (Seller drain protection).

### 3.5 Marketplace escrow interaction with the TBA

- Before listing the seller withdraws from their PersonalAccount if they wish (keyed to them, not the NFT) and unwinds positions. `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.3 Transfer design` (steps 1 to 3).
- `AgentEscrow.list(agentId, price, expectedSkills)` in one transaction: moves the NFT into escrow (epoch bumps; the Executor rejects every old session, pending intent and PersonalAccount trading action); checks and reverts unless `tba.isLocked() == false`, `tba.extsload(ERC1967_SLOT) == 0x41C8...`, the TBA holds `expectedSkills`, and `BuildRegistry.buildHash(agentId)` matches; snapshots `tba.state()`. `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.3 Transfer design` (steps 4 and 5).
- During the listing nobody can execute from the TBA (the escrow never calls it); the seller can cancel, which returns the NFT and bumps the epoch again, so the Executor does not revive old sessions even though Tokenbound's grants come back. `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.3 Transfer design` (steps 6 to 8).
- `AgentEscrow.buy(agentId)` re-checks state, lock, implementation, skills and build hash, pays the seller, transfers the NFT to the buyer, and bumps the epoch. `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.3 Transfer design` (step 9).
- The buyer then confirms or re-sets the build (config epoch bump), creates their PersonalAccount, and registers a new session key for the new epoch with a Privy policy restricted to the Executor. `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.3 Transfer design` (steps 10 and 11).
- Escrow reads from the TBA: `isLocked`, `extsload`, `state`. It never writes. `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.4 Recommended contract design` (Components).
- Sales outside the escrow are not protected against seller drain; the UI must say so, and AgentNFT transfers can be restricted to approved operators. `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.3 Transfer design` (Sales outside our escrow).

### 3.6 Ownership epochs and the TBA

- The TBA has no epoch, nonce or owner-change hook; Tokenbound grants are owner-keyed and revive on return. Our epoch model lives entirely in our contracts: `ownerEpoch` in AgentNFT, `configEpoch` in BuildRegistry, both carried in every Executor intent and checked by Executor, BuildRegistry and PersonalAccount. The epoch must bump on every transfer, not on "owner differs from previous owner". `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.2 Component mapping` (Old permissions stop on transfer); `notes/A-account-core.md > Implications for our platform` (3); `notes/D-security.md > 7`.
- Session keys are authorized in the Executor keyed by `(agentId, ownerEpoch)`, never via `setPermissions`. `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.2 Component mapping` (Session key authorization).
- Interface sketch: `IExecutor.SwapIntent { agentId, ownerEpoch, configEpoch, tokenIn, tokenOut, amountIn, minOut, deadline, nonce }`; `registerSession(agentId, key, expiry)` (msg.sender == ownerOf); `revokeSession(agentId, key)`; `executeSwap(intent)` (msg.sender == session key). `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.4 Recommended contract design` (Interface sketches).
- `PersonalAccount` (one clone per `(agentId, owner)`): `owner()` fixed at creation; `withdraw(token, amount, to)` owner only, always on, no pause, no Executor dependency; `trade(router, tokenIn, amountIn, swapData)` requires `msg.sender == executor`, `AgentNFT.ownerOf(agentId) == owner`, and a matching epoch, so trading stops by itself when the NFT changes hands. `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.4 Recommended contract design` (Interface sketches).
- Hard-limit enforcement table (which contract enforces allowed assets, single venue, 10% per trade, 40% concentration, 10% USDC floor, 0.5% slippage, exact approvals, 2-minute deadline, drawdown breaker at 10% and 20%, no leverage, always-on withdrawals, session key reach, epochs, skill slots). `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.4 Recommended contract design` (Which contract enforces each hard limit).
- StrategyVault (ERC-4626): manager is the Executor acting for `agentId` at the current epoch; the TBA gets no vault role and never custodies vault assets; the manager has no withdrawal path. On an `ownerEpoch` change the vault pauses new manager actions for a cooldown, depositors can redeem, and the new owner must accept the manager role. `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.2 Component mapping` (Interaction with the strategy vault); `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.4 Recommended contract design` (StrategyVault on manager change).

### 3.7 What a custom account implementation would need (Option C)

- Ownership epoch inside the account; a scoped executor module; no overrides; lock-aware and domain-separated ERC-1271 (an ERC-7739-style wrapper); no upgradability or our own guardian. Cost: a new implementation plus an audit; the SDK only works with a custom `implementationAddress` and does no auto-initialize; it loses canonical tooling and marketplace recognition. `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.5 Use unmodified, extend, or replace` (Option C); `notes/D-security.md > Findings table` (F1, F2, F10 mitigations); `notes/C-sdk.md > 2. Mechanisms` (Account creation).
- Specific behaviors a custom `_handleOverride` and `_isValidSignature` would need: respect `isLocked()`, return false while locked. `notes/D-security.md > Findings table` (F1, F2).

### 3.8 Executor constraints that come from the TBA analysis

- The Executor must not implement ERC-1271 `isValidSignature` (or must reject calls about TBAs), because a permissioned contract that approves hashes becomes a TBA signer through the `v == 0` path. Not needed in the recommended design since the Executor is never permissioned, but it is a standing rule. `notes/A-account-core.md > Implications for our platform` (2); `notes/D-security.md > 5.1`.
- Reentrancy guards belong in the Executor. `notes/D-security.md > 4.3`.
- Exact approvals consumed and zeroed in the same transaction (`approve(router, amountIn)`, swap, `approve(router, 0)`); never approve Permit2. `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.4 Recommended contract design` (Which contract enforces each hard limit); `notes/D-security.md > 6. Approvals left behind`.

### 3.9 ERC-8004, x402, ERC-4337, SDK

- ERC-8004: register the agent with `tokenId`, AgentNFT and the TBA address as its agent wallet. If ERC-8004 checks the wallet's signature, the TBA's ERC-1271 accepts the owner's signature (**Inferred**; check against the spec). `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.2 Component mapping` (ERC-8004 identity binding); `03-spike-plan.md > 10. Open questions` (8).
- x402: autonomous TBA signing would need a permissioned signer with full power, so pay x402 from the operating wallet with EIP-3009; use TBA ERC-1271 only for owner-signed identity messages. Generic `personal_sign` messages replay across all TBAs of the same owner, so identity payloads must bind the account. `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.2 Component mapping` (ERC-1271 signing for x402); `notes/A-account-core.md > Implications for our platform` (6).
- ERC-4337: sponsor the Privy wallets (owner and session key) calling our contracts; do not make the TBA the UserOp sender; confirm Privy's EntryPoint version. `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.2 Component mapping` (ERC-4337 and gas sponsorship).
- SDK: `TokenboundClient` with `chain: monad`, plus our own viem helpers for `setPermissions`, `lock`, `state`, `extsload` and `overrides` reads; pin the version; keep our own address constants. `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.2 Component mapping` (SDK for the frontend); `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.6 Risks, ranked` (10).
- Operating wallet is Privy-managed and pays gas, inference credits and x402. `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.4 Recommended contract design` (Components).
- Risks ranked 1 to 12 with mitigations. `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.6 Risks, ranked`.

## 6. Gaps and weak spots

- Everything that shapes the escrow design is still Inferred. S1 (override drain), S2 (signature drain on USDC), S5 (revival), S6, S11 and the non-idempotent SDK creation were derived by reading code and were never executed; Foundry was not installed. `01-codebase-report.md` header; `03-spike-plan.md` header. The register's mainnet launch depends on 6B and 6D in particular.
- Internal contradiction on the implementation check. `notes/B-registry-deploy-trust.md > 2. Upgradeability` says an escrow contract cannot read another contract's storage slot and would need a lock-free design, while `01-codebase-report.md > 5.2 Upgradeability` and `notes/D-security.md > 7` say `extsload` is a public view any contract can call. The final report's position is the later one; spike 9b must confirm it before the escrow depends on it.
- The escrow itself becomes the TBA's root owner during a listing. The research only says "the escrow never calls it" (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.3` step 6). Inference: the escrow must have no generic call path and must not implement ERC-1271, because `_isValidSigner` resolves to the escrow and the `v == 0` path would make an ERC-1271-capable escrow a valid TBA signer (`notes/D-security.md > 5.1`). This is a hard design constraint on the escrow that the research does not state.
- The escrow's pinned implementation `0x41C8` is a constant in the design (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.3` step 5). Inference: if the Tokenbound Safe ever trusts a new implementation and owners upgrade legitimately, every upgraded agent becomes unlistable until the escrow is changed. An allowlist we govern avoids that.
- Monitoring the guardian on Monad is required (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.6` rank 5) but the public RPC caps `eth_getLogs` at 100 blocks and no Monad explorer API worked (`notes/B-registry-deploy-trust.md > 3`; `03-spike-plan.md > 10` Q4). The plan needs an indexer for guardian, `PermissionUpdated` and `OverrideUpdated` events; nothing covers this.
- A custom account later is not a drop-in. The TBA address changes with the implementation argument, `upgradeTo` needs Tokenbound's trust, and the SDK will not initialize a custom implementation (`notes/B-registry-deploy-trust.md > 1`, `> 2`; `notes/C-sdk.md > 2`). The register assumes a later switch without a migration story.
- The research's claim that a custom account is what enables open-marketplace sales is incomplete. Inference from `notes/D-security.md > 1.2`: even with S1 to S3 closed, the seller can move skills out by plain `execute` right before a fill unless the marketplace pins `state()` or the account is locked, and no Monad marketplace is known to do either (`notes/D-security.md > Open questions` 7).
- The escrow's `state()` snapshot and re-check (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.3` steps 5 and 9) is redundant while the escrow is the root owner, since only the root owner or its permissioned addresses can bump state (Inference from `notes/A-account-core.md > 4. state()`). Harmless, but it should not be mistaken for the protection; custody is.
- SkillRegistry, WorkflowNFT, ProtocolRegistry and adapters, and Billing are absent from the research. WorkflowNFT in particular has no custody model; if it lives in the TBA it inherits every finding above (Inference).
- Slot cost and content-hash versions (register) are not what BuildRegistry sketches (`uint32` versions, count-based slots) (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.4`). Inference: the BuildRegistry interface must be redrawn.
- The identity of Monad USDC is inferred, not confirmed (`01-codebase-report.md > 1.2`). S2's severity for USDC depends on it.
- The Privy EntryPoint version, bundler acceptance of TBA UserOps, and whether Privy policies can restrict a key to a single contract are all outside the code and unresolved (`notes/B-registry-deploy-trust.md > 4`; `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.2`).
- The oracle for the 10%, 40% and 10% USDC limits and the drawdown breaker is unchosen (`03-spike-plan.md > 10` Q9). The Executor cannot be specified without it.
- The SDK adds little for this design. The only V3 functions the platform would use are `getAccount` and `createAccount`, and mint creation happens onchain in AgentNFT anyway (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.2`). Inference: a small viem module with copied constants and the V3 ABI may be simpler than depending on an unmaintained package whose root exports are V2 (`notes/C-sdk.md > 5` #1, #21).
- Monad testnet cannot rehearse guardian changes because the owning Safe has no code there (`notes/B-registry-deploy-trust.md > 3`).
- The 35 commits after the Zellic audit, including `FxChildExecutor`, are unaudited, and the seller-as-attacker model was never audited (`notes/D-security.md > 8.1`). The research accepts this for a skills-only role; the acceptance should be written down.
- Owners can lock their own TBA (up to 365 days) and cannot then revoke permissions or set overrides (`notes/A-account-core.md > 7. Lock`). Inference: the UI should never offer `lock()`, and support flows should expect agents that are unlistable for the lock period.
- Monad SELFDESTRUCT semantics (EIP-6780) are unknown and touch sandbox and override edge cases (`03-spike-plan.md > 10` Q13; `notes/D-security.md > Open questions` 6).

## 7. Open questions needing a human decision

1. Is `0x754704Bc...b603` Circle's official USDC on Monad, FiatToken v2.2 with ERC-1271 in `permit` and `transferWithAuthorization`? Recommended: confirm with Circle before mainnet and treat S2 as applying until then. Reason: it decides whether USDC can ever sit in a TBA and which token x402 uses (`03-spike-plan.md > 10` Q1).
2. Which EntryPoint versions does Privy support on Monad, and do its bundlers accept TBA UserOps? Recommended: design so the answer does not matter, with the TBA never a UserOp sender and sponsorship on Privy wallets calling our contracts. Reason: the TBA is v0.6-only and strict bundlers may reject it (`03-spike-plan.md > 10` Q2; `notes/B-registry-deploy-trust.md > 4`).
3. Will Tokenbound's Safe trust new implementations on Monad, and will it announce them? Recommended: ask, and regardless run an indexer on the guardian and keep an escrow implementation allowlist. Reason: an upgrade survives transfer and is invisible to `state()` (`03-spike-plan.md > 10` Q3; `notes/B-registry-deploy-trust.md > Open questions` 1, 2).
4. Guardian event history on Monad: accept the Safe-nonce-0 argument or scan events? Recommended: accept for now, scan once an indexer exists. Reason: the nonce argument is high confidence but indirect (`03-spike-plan.md > 10` Q4).
5. Does the nested root-owner walk fail for canonical proxy accounts, and may agents ever own agents? Recommended: run 9a, and forbid agent-in-TBA nesting at launch regardless. Reason: if nesting half-works, grants keyed to a parent TBA survive a sale (`03-spike-plan.md > 10` Q5; `notes/A-account-core.md > 6`).
6. Is there a newer `@tokenbound/sdk` or contracts release with Monad support or a changed `AccountProxy`? Recommended: check read-only, then either pin 0.5.5 or drop the SDK for our own viem module. Reason: the root exports are V2 and the package is unmaintained (`03-spike-plan.md > 10` Q6; `notes/C-sdk.md > 5`).
7. Does Anvil fork Monad reliably? Recommended: run spike 0 first; if it fails, run the same tests against testnet deployments of our mocks. Reason: every spike depends on it (`03-spike-plan.md > 10` Q7).
8. How does ERC-8004 bind an agent wallet, and does it verify through ERC-1271? Recommended: read the spec and the Monad registry deployment before binding the TBA; require any signed payload to include the TBA address. Reason: TBA-as-wallet is Inferred and raw-hash ERC-1271 replays across an owner's TBAs (`03-spike-plan.md > 10` Q8; `notes/D-security.md > 5.1`).
9. Which price oracle backs the Executor limits? Recommended: separate research; do not decide here. Reason: outside Tokenbound scope (`03-spike-plan.md > 10` Q9).
10. Should AgentNFT transfers be restricted to the escrow and approved operators onchain? Recommended: yes at launch. Reason: the register says escrow-only, and a UI warning does not stop seller drain on a generic marketplace (`03-spike-plan.md > 10` Q10; `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.3`).
11. StrategyVault handover policy on sale: cooldown plus depositor redeem window plus manager acceptance (research) versus "leader changes apply only to new deposits" (register)? Recommended: combine them, with a redeem window for existing depositors and the new leader managing only post-acceptance deposits, and decide what happens to old deposits that are not redeemed. Reason: the two texts describe different mechanisms and neither alone covers existing deposits (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.4`; `03-spike-plan.md > 10` Q11).
12. Accept the 35 unaudited commits and the missing seller-as-attacker audit model for a skills-only TBA? Recommended: accept, in writing, and revisit only if capital ever enters the TBA. Reason: the research's own recommendation (`03-spike-plan.md > 10` Q12; `notes/D-security.md > 8.1`).
13. Does Monad implement EIP-6780 SELFDESTRUCT semantics? Recommended: check Monad docs; low priority for skills-only. Reason: affects only sandbox and override forging edge cases (`03-spike-plan.md > 10` Q13).
14. Must agent TBA addresses stay stable if we later move to a custom account? Recommended: decide now; if yes, weigh starting with a custom implementation behind our own proxy against a planned migration. Reason: a different implementation argument is a different address and `upgradeTo` needs Tokenbound's trust (`notes/B-registry-deploy-trust.md > 1`, `> 2`).
15. Is the escrow's expected implementation a constant or an allowlist we govern? Recommended: allowlist with a single entry at launch. Reason: avoids blocking legitimately upgraded agents later (Inference on `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.3`).
16. Should the platform ever grant a TBA permission to the Executor (Option B)? Recommended: no. Reason: the register and the research agree, and a permissioned contract can upgrade and sign (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.5`).
17. Which viem version, and does the SDK dist bundle `viem/chains`? Recommended: use a viem that exports `monad` and `monadTestnet`, with `defineChain` as fallback, and inspect the dist in a scratch project. Reason: the lockfile viem lacks Monad (`notes/C-sdk.md > Open questions` 1, 2).
18. Do our Uniswap routes require Permit2? Recommended: choose routes that take direct router approvals; never approve Permit2 from any account we control on behalf of users. Reason: Permit2 signatures lack an `owner` field and replay across accounts (`notes/D-security.md > Open questions` 5; `notes/D-security.md > 5.1`).
19. Custody model for WorkflowNFT. Recommended: decide whether it lives in the TBA (then it needs SkillNFT-style transfer restrictions) or elsewhere, before writing the escrow. Reason: the research never saw it (Inference).
