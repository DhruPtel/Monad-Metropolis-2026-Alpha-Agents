Versions, licenses, audit status, summaries`; `00-phase0-orientation.md > hypurrquant map`). Overall verdict (`02-platform-mapping.md > Bottom line`): neither repository has anything like our offline in-kind exit, and both let an operator freeze every exit, so on that point both conflict with our non-negotiable rule and our design is stricter; the typed-intent Executor holds up because Veda itself built a typed swap layer (BoringSwapper) on top of Merkle to get amounts, oracles and rate limits; live oracle valuation holds up over a pushed exchange rate; and the repositories mostly improve our design in details: where delays are enforced (in contract code, hypurrquant style), who can mint and burn (only the vault itself), how swaps are checked after execution (realized balance delta against an oracle, BoringSwapper style), how lockups are set (per deposit, hard maximum, no third-party relock), and how exits survive token-level failures (skip or credit a reverting token). From hypurrquant it says to borrow the in-code propose/accept timelock, the per-user cost-basis performance fee with fee-rate snapshot and fee-change timelock, accrued fees excluded from NAV, `receiver == controller` for operator-triggered exits, `receiver == msg.sender` on deposits, reserved balances, and the mocked-oracle test technique; and to avoid failed reads counting as zero, instant role grants that undercut a timelock, and keeper-gated pausable redemption (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.1 Design comparison table`; `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.2 Recommended changes (ranked)`).

## 2. Facts the final plan must carry

### 2.1 Custody and modules

- The BoringVault core is a solmate ERC-20 share token that also holds every asset; the only state beyond ERC20 and Auth is one `hook` variable. It exposes `manage` (arbitrary call with any target, calldata and native value), `enter` (pull an asset and mint a caller-chosen share amount, no pricing), `exit` (burn from any `from` with no allowance check, then transfer assets anywhere), `setBeforeTransferHook`, `receive()`, and ERC721 and ERC1155 receivers. All three value-moving functions are gated only by `requiresAuth`. The vault does no pricing, has no pause and no limits, and is not upgradeable (`A-custody-modules.md > 1.1 Every external function`; `01-codebase-report.md > 3.1 The BoringVault core`).
- The transfer hook is called only on `transfer` and `transferFrom`, never on mint or burn, and it is a `view` call, so it can only revert (`A-custody-modules.md > 1.1 Every external function`).
- solmate `Auth.isAuthorized` asks the authority before checking the owner, so a reverting authority bricks every gated function for everyone; `setAuthority` swaps the whole permission table in one call. `RolesAuthority.canCall` is per (target contract, 4-byte selector) with no argument checks and no built-in delay (`A-custody-modules.md > 2.1 Auth`; `A-custody-modules.md > 2.2 RolesAuthority`).
- Deploy-script role wiring: Manager holds MANAGER (1) and MANAGER_INTERNAL (4); Teller holds MINTER (2) and BURNER (3); QueueSolver holds SOLVER (12) and CAN_SOLVE (31); OWNER (8) controls `setManageRoot`, `setBeforeTransferHook`, `setAuthority` and fee setters; PAUSER (5) controls pause and unpause. Every module's `owner` is then set to zero, so the owner of the RolesAuthority is the real root of trust (`A-custody-modules.md > 2.3 Role ids and selector map (deployment script)`; `A-custody-modules.md > 2.4 End state after setup: owner is zero, RolesAuthority is the root`).
- Live Monad vmUSD: every module `owner()` is zero; the RolesAuthority owner is `0x16ba7650...`, a contract with `getMinDelay() = 172,800 s` (48 h), inferred to be an OpenZeppelin TimelockController; only that timelock can change roles. The Teller holds MANAGER_ROLE because `TellerWithYieldStreaming` inherits `TellerWithBuffer`, whose hooks call `vault.manage` with helper-built calldata, a second path to `manage` that bypasses Merkle verification. Teller `deposit` and `withdraw` are public; `bulkWithdraw` and `boringRedeemSelfSolve` are not; `shareLockPeriod` is 15 s. The repo config records no timelock (`Timelock: 0x0`, `shouldDeploy: false`), yet a 48 h timelock owns the authority live, so repo config does not match onchain state (`A-custody-modules.md > 2.5 Live Monad vmUSD wiring (read onchain, V)`; `00-phase0-orientation.md > Addendum: live vmUSD role reads on Monad`; `01-codebase-report.md > 3.3 Live Monad vmUSD wiring`).
- No BoringVault contract enforces a delay. Observed delays are a deployment convention: 0 s (Plasma `DeployTimelock.s.sol`), 300 s (eBTC `setRoleCapability` batch), 48 h (live vmUSD) (`A-custody-modules.md > 2.7 Timelock usage`; `01-codebase-report.md > 3.4 Timelocks are a convention, not a contract property`).
- `Pauser` fans pause calls out to Teller, Queue, Accountant and Manager; pause and unpause are separate roles; sender-scoped pause lets a bot pause only its own module. The core vault has no pause (`A-custody-modules.md > 2.6 Pauser`).
- Ways an exit can be blocked in BoringVault: Teller paused; Accountant paused, including auto-pause on an out-of-bounds rate; `allowWithdraws` false for the asset; user deny-listed; Teller loses BURNER or a new Teller is swapped in; authority swapped or reverting; hook set to a reverting contract; withdraw not made public. There is no in-kind exit and no exit that avoids the Accountant (`A-custody-modules.md > 4. Can a user exit if admins misbehave? (interaction with redeemInKind)`; `01-codebase-report.md > 3.5 What can block an exit in BoringVault`).
- `TellerWithRemediation` can freeze a user, then after 3 days burn their shares and mint them to an admin-chosen address; `refundDeposit` burns shares within the lock window; `BoringOnChainQueue.rescueTokens` cannot take shares backing active requests (`D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 4.4 Can admins seize or redirect shares? (V)`).
- Module constructors bind the vault immutably, so a module serves one vault, but a vault can be served by any module the authority allows (`A-custody-modules.md > 3. Who can change which module is attached`).
- Value can leave the vault through modules: BoringSwapper limit orders escrow principal in the swapper (`pendingOrderPrincipal`), and `TellerWithBuffer` parks idle funds in ERC-4626 or lending buffers via `vault.manage` (`B-strategy-restrictions.md > 4.3 BoringSwapper: Veda's typed swap layer`; `D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 9.4 TellerWithBuffer (V)`).
- What the tiny-custody split buys: a small audited surface for funds, immutable custody with swappable logic and no proxy, least privilege per module, an onchain per-selector audit trail. What it costs: the generic `manage` makes custody unconstrained, the wiring is mutable with delay by convention only, exits depend on at least two modules, and the setup is complex enough that Monad config drifted from live state (`A-custody-modules.md > 5.1 Gains`; `A-custody-modules.md > 5.2 Costs`).

### 2.2 Strategy restrictions

- Leaf encoding: `keccak256(abi.encodePacked(decoderAndSanitizer, target, valueNonZero, selector, packedArgumentAddresses))`. The root is per caller (`manageRoot[msg.sender]`); `setManageRoot` is `requiresAuth` (OWNER_ROLE) and immediate (`B-strategy-restrictions.md > 1.2 Leaf structure (exact encoding)`; `B-strategy-restrictions.md > 1.5 Roots per strategist`).
- Decoders mirror the target ABI, are `staticcall`ed with the real calldata and return the packed sensitive addresses; unknown selectors hit a fallback that reverts (fail closed). Sanitization examples: Uniswap V3 path length must be `20 mod 23`; the V4 decoder accepts only one swap plus `SETTLE_ALL` and `TAKE_ALL`. "Owned" decoders keep a mutable executor address that changes permissions without a root update (`B-strategy-restrictions.md > 1.3 How decoders extract sensitive arguments`).
- A leaf can force recipient = vault, pin the tokens and direction, and forbid native value. It cannot bind `amountIn`, `amountOutMinimum`, fee tier, deadline, or approval amount (`B-strategy-restrictions.md > 2. Can a leaf force recipient = vault, restrict tokens, forbid native value?`).
- What Merkle cannot express: percentage of vault per trade, max percentage per asset or USDC floor, oracle price and freshness, slippage, rate limits, deadlines, epochs (only partially, by replacing a root), exact approvals with reset, native value amount (`B-strategy-restrictions.md > 4.1 Gaps`).
- The only post-call check in the Manager is that `vault.totalSupply()` is unchanged; Manager pause blocks strategist calls only, not deposits or withdrawals (`B-strategy-restrictions.md > 1.7 Pause and post-call checks`).
- Flash-loan inner calls are verified against the Manager's own shared root, not the strategist's (`B-strategy-restrictions.md > 1.6 Flash loan path`).
- Micro-managers hold STRATEGIST_ROLE with their own narrow root; a bot gets a role on the micro-manager, which builds calldata itself. `DexSwapperUManager` hardcodes recipient = vault, approves exactly `amountIn`, measures the output balance delta, checks it against a `PriceRouter` within `allowedSlippage` (default 5 bps, max 10%), and revokes leftover allowance (`B-strategy-restrictions.md > 3.2 The micro-managers`).
- `UManager.enforceRateLimit` is broken: it keys the counter on `block.timestamp % period` (the second-offset within a period, not the period index), counters never reset, `period` is `uint16` so 24 h cannot be configured, and it degrades into permanent denial. Its tests exercise one timestamp only (`B-strategy-restrictions.md > 4.2 UManager.enforceRateLimit: not a rolling window, and actually broken`).
- BoringSwapper: `SwapConfig = {tokenRoute{tokenIn, tokenOut}, adapter, quoteAsset, swapData, slippageBps, receiver}`. Pre-flight: adapter registered, approved and unpaused; the adapter is staticcalled with the router calldata and must agree with the typed config (for example `UniswapV3Adapter.exactInput` requires router recipient = swapper and path endpoints = tokenIn and tokenOut). Rate limit: token bucket per directed route, in token units, not count and not percent of NAV; `setRouteConfig` refills the bucket. Post-flight: pull exactly `amount` from the vault into the swapper, `approve(0)` then `approve(amount)`, call the router, `approve(0)` then restore the prior allowance, measure the realized `tokenOut` delta, `PriceValidator.validate` requires every oracle valuation of the output to be at least every valuation of the input times `(1 - slippageBps)`, send output to `receiver`, return dust. No deadline is enforced; staleness is delegated to the rate provider (6 h in the Monad test script) (`B-strategy-restrictions.md > 4.3 BoringSwapper: Veda's typed swap layer (the real answer to the gaps)`; `01-codebase-report.md > 4.4 What Merkle cannot express, and Veda's patches`).
- Live Monad test swapper `0x6b01D470...`: directed routes with 5 bps on stable pairs and 1000 bps on volatile ones, volume buckets of about 2m per 24 h on stable routes; inferred to be a test deployment (`B-strategy-restrictions.md > 4.4 Monad: SwapperTestVault root and live swapper config`).
- `FeeRegistry.maxFeeBps` can be set up to 100%; it is a swap fee, not a vault fee (`B-strategy-restrictions.md > 4.3 BoringSwapper: Veda's typed swap layer (the real answer to the gaps)`; `C-accounting.md > 3.3 FeeRegistry`).
- Reduce-only can be expressed as a directional one-way route set (non-USDC to USDC only) held by a separate role, rather than a flag in the trading path (`B-strategy-restrictions.md > 3.3 Mapping to our roles`).
- hypurrquant enforces no trade size, asset, slippage, count or loss limit onchain; trading is offchain through a HyperCore agent wallet. Onchain keeper actions only move USDC between the vault's own accounts, cannot touch `reservedEvmBalance`, and bridging requires `NavLib.checkPriceSanity` (5% divergence) (`B-strategy-restrictions.md > 6. hypurrquant: how the keeper is restricted`; `01-codebase-report.md > 4.6 hypurrquant keeper restrictions`).

### 2.3 Accounting

- `AccountantWithRateProviders` stores an exchange rate pushed by UPDATE_EXCHANGE_RATE_ROLE (an offchain bot); the contract never reads balances. Bounds are a minimum delay between updates plus upper and lower bps of the previous rate. A violating update is not rejected: it is stored, fee accrual is skipped, and the Accountant pauses. While paused `getRate()` still returns the stored (possibly bad) rate but `getRateSafe` and `getRateInQuoteSafe` revert; the Teller and Queue use only the safe variants, so deposits, withdrawals, new queue requests and `claimFees` all stop until PAUSER unpauses. The bound is per update, not per unit of time (`C-accounting.md > 2.2 Who publishes the rate and how often`; `C-accounting.md > 2.3 Bounds and what happens on violation`; `C-accounting.md > 2.4 What pause affects, and who can unpause`).
- Fee formulas: `S_use = min(totalSupply_now, totalSharesLastUpdate)`; platform fee on `S_use * min(R_old, R_new)`, time-prorated; performance fee `(R_new - HWM) * S_use * bps` when `R_new > HWM`; the HWM is global on the share price and is raised even when the fee rate is zero; `resetHighwaterMark` (OWNER) lowers it to the current rate after a drawdown; caps 20% platform, 50% performance; `feesOwedInBase` is a liability not netted from the rate onchain; `claimFees` must be called by the vault itself (`C-accounting.md > 2.5 Fee formulas (Verified, lines 541-621)`; `C-accounting.md > 2.6 claimFees and fee-asset conversion`).
- Multi-asset support only converts the single pushed share price into another asset via a rate provider; `setRateProviderData` has no in-contract delay (`C-accounting.md > 2.7 Multi-asset rates`).
- `GenericRateProviderWithStalenessCheck` enforces staleness and negative-answer rejection and forces 18 decimals, but has no deviation check, no min or max answer, no sequencer check (`C-accounting.md > 2.8 GenericRateProviderWithStalenessCheck`).
- `AccountantWithYieldStreaming` (live vmUSD): the strategist posts `vestYield(amount, duration)`, price rises linearly; `postLoss` applies losses immediately and pauses above `maxDeviationLoss`; the base `updateExchangeRate(uint96)` is overridden to revert, so the configured plus or minus 0.2% bounds are inert; deposit rounding divides by `rate + 1`. Live reads: `platformFee = 0` and `performanceFee = 0` although the repo config says 50 bps platform fee (`C-accounting.md > 3.1 Yield streaming`).
- hypurrquant `totalAssets() = evmBalance + coreNAV + inflightBuffer - accruedFees`, computed live on every call. Every precompile read returns `(false, 0)` on failure and counts as zero, so a failed read understates NAV and a deposit in that block mints too many shares (`C-accounting.md > 4.1 totalAssets`).
- hypurrquant virtual offset `VIRTUAL_SHARES = VIRTUAL_ASSETS = 1e6` with Floor in both conversion directions; cost-basis moves use Ceil in the user's favor so rounding does not become a fee overcharge; the research calls 1e6/1e6 a weak offset (`C-accounting.md > 4.3 Virtual offset and rounding (Verified)`).
- `NavLib.checkPriceSanity` reverts deposits when perp oracle vs mark, or spot vs perp oracle, diverge more than 5%; it applies to deposits only, not to redeem pricing (`C-accounting.md > 4.4 Deposit gating on price divergence`).
- Accrued but unclaimed fees are subtracted from `totalAssets` the moment they are recognized; `claimFees` pays only from free balance (`C-accounting.md > 4.5 Accrued fees excluded from NAV`).
- hypurrquant performance fee: default 10%, max 30%; changes proposed then active after a 24 h `FEE_CHANGE_TIMELOCK`, activation permissionless; per-user `userDeposits` cost basis that migrates pro rata on share transfers; principal snapshot at `requestRedeem`; fee rate snapshot at `markRedeemReady`; `fee = max(0, released - principal) * feeBpsSnapshot / 10000` per request; `realizedPnL` is tracked but never used, so there is no loss carry-forward; `emergencyRedeem` caps payout at principal; a natspec comment contradicts the snapshot behavior and the code is authoritative (`C-accounting.md > 4.6 Performance fee: per-user cost basis (Verified)`).
- Global HWM versus per-user basis: the global HWM is unfair across cohorts and its reset is manager-favorable; the per-user model matches Hyperliquid's leader fee as the research understands it but needs a price at exit, which conflicts with an oracle-free in-kind exit unless a stored price is used (`C-accounting.md > 5. Per-user vs global high-water mark (for the future 10% fee)`).
- The share lock in BoringVault exists to stop deposit-then-exit arbitrage against a stale, stepwise pushed rate; the same fix applies to oracle lag (`D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 3.2 What problem it solves (I, strongly supported)`).

### 2.4 Deposits and withdrawals

- Teller deposit paths: `deposit(asset, amount, minimumMint, referral)` is public only if configured and locks `msg.sender`; `deposit(..., to, referral)` is role-gated because a third party depositing to `to` would reset the victim's share lock; `depositWithPermit` tries the permit and falls back to the existing allowance so a front-run permit cannot DoS the deposit; `bulkDeposit` (SOLVER) sets no lock; native deposits wrap via a sentinel address (`D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 2.1 Entry points`).
- Share math: `shares = amount * ONE_SHARE / getRateInQuoteSafe(asset)`, then a per-asset `sharePremium` haircut (max 10%), a `minimumMint` check, and an optional share-supply `depositCap` (`uint112`, no partial fills, no per-user cap) (`D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 2.2 Share math and premium`; `D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 2.3 Deposit cap`).
- Teller `pause` blocks deposits and withdrawals even though its comment says deposits only. Closing to new deposits is only possible by pause, per-asset `allowDeposits`, or a cap (`D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 2.4 Pause`; `D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 2.5 Close to new deposits`).
- Deny lists live in the Teller's `beforeTransfer` hook; a `denyFrom` user cannot move shares into the queue and so cannot exit at all (`D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 2.6 Deny lists and the beforeTransfer hook`).
- Share lock: `shareLockPeriod` bounded by `MAX_SHARE_LOCK_PERIOD = 3 days`; `_afterPublicDeposit` sets `shareUnlockTime = now + period` for the whole address (per address, not per lot) and stores a deposit hash for refunds; while locked, transfers and `withdraw` revert; lowering the period lets pending locks escape by re-depositing 1 wei; a zero period allows mint and transfer in the same transaction (`D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 3.1 Mechanism (V)`).
- `refundDeposit` (STRATEGIST_MULTISIG) burns the minted shares and returns the original deposit while the lock is active; the research does not recommend it for a vault run by an untrusted manager (`D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 3.3 Refund (V)`; `D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 3.4 Relevance to us`).
- `withdraw(asset, shares, minAssets, to)` is `requiresAuth`, never made public by the deploy script (only tests do), but is public on live Monad vmUSD; `_withdraw` reverts if the Teller is paused, the asset disallows withdrawals, or the Accountant is paused; payout is single-asset at the stored rate and reverts when idle balance is short unless a buffer helper pulls funds (`D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 4.1 Teller exit functions (V)`; `00-phase0-orientation.md > Addendum: live vmUSD role reads on Monad`).
- Queue: `requestOnChainWithdraw(asset, shares, discount, secondsToDeadline)` escrows shares and fixes the payout at request time using the rate minus a user discount (max 30%); maturity and minimum deadline can each be up to 30 days; only a hash is stored and recovery is the indexer's job; `cancelOnChainWithdraw` is not pause-gated; `withdrawCapacity` throttles; self-solve is public only if configured (not on vmUSD) and cannot cover a deficit; `excessToSolverNonSelfSolve` is true for Monad vmUSD (`D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 5.1 Configuration per withdraw asset (V)`; `D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 5.2 Request lifecycle (V)`; `D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 5.3 Solver (V)`).
- Failure matrix for a lone user's exit: Teller pause, Accountant pause (manual or automatic), queue pause, asset stopped, capacity zero, deny list, self-solve not public, no idle balance of the asset, rate fell more than the discount, deadline passed, `cancelUserWithdraws` by a multisig, long maturity constants (`D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 4.3 Can a user exit alone? Failure matrix (V unless marked)`).
- Archived `DelayedWithdraw` pays `shares * min(rateAtRequest, rateNow)` and is user-completable after maturity; `AtomicQueue` is an approval-based OTC book that needs a solver (`D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 6.1 src/archive/DelayedWithdraw.sol (V)`; `D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 6.2 src/archive/atomic-queue/AtomicQueue.sol (V)`).
- hypurrquant ERC-7540 lifecycle: `requestRedeem` requires `controller == owner`, at most 50 active requests, shares moved into the vault, cost basis moved to `lockedPrincipal`, redeem timelock snapshotted per request (default 24 h, min 1 h, max 7 days); a 10-minute grace before the keeper may `markRedeemReady`, which reserves `convertToAssets(shares)` from free EVM USDC and snapshots the fee rate; `redeem` is FIFO across ready requests with partial consumption of the last; operators must pay to `receiver == controller`; cancel (ERC-7887) is not pause-gated; `revertRedeemReady` and `emergencyRedeem` (capped at principal) are admin-only. Ready shares stay in `totalSupply` and reserved USDC stays in `totalAssets` until claim, so remaining holders carry NAV moves in between (`D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 7.2 Lifecycle (V)`; `D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 7.3 Notable details`).
- hypurrquant deposit caps: `maxTotalDeposit` against `totalAssets`, `maxUserDeposit` including locked principal, and `deposit` requires `receiver == msg.sender` (`D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 7.3 Notable details`; `D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 9.1 Caps`).
- Withdrawals larger than idle: BoringVault reverts or pulls from a buffer; the queue waits for a solver; hypurrquant waits for the keeper; neither has proportional closing (`D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 8.1 How each system handles it`).
- Leader stake: neither repository has any concept of a manager or leader stake (`D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 9.5 Leader stake enforcement`).
- Tests: BoringVault tests are mostly mainnet forks with an invariant suite in `test/fuzzing/` (`noFreeAssets`, `dustFavorsTheHouse`, `vaultSolvencyMulti`, `tellerPaused_methodsRevert`, `zeroAllowanceOnAssets`, `highwaterMarkNeverDecreases`, and others) and no queue invariants; hypurrquant has about 157 local unit tests with HyperCore mocked via `vm.mockCall` at fixed precompile addresses, two fuzz tests and no invariants (`D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 10.1 BoringVault`; `D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 10.2 hypurrquant`).

### 2.5 hypurrquant

- What it is: a single UUPS-upgradeable `UsdcVault` (ERC-4626 plus ERC-7540 async redeem plus ERC-7887 cancel) on HyperEVM (chain 999) that auto-bridges deposits to the vault's own HyperCore account, where an offchain keeper trades a delta-neutral spot and perp position through an agent wallet that can trade but not withdraw; `totalAssets()` is read live from precompiles; logic is split into delegatecalled libraries only to fit the contract size limit (`01-codebase-report.md > 1. Versions, licenses, audit status, summaries`; `A-custody-modules.md > 6. hypurrquant HyperVault structure`).
- Roles: `DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE`, `ADMIN_ROLE`, `KEEPER_ROLE`, `GUARDIAN_ROLE`; guardian pauses, admin unpauses; `pause` blocks `deposit`, `requestRedeem` and `redeem`. Upgrade timelock enforced in code: `proposeUpgrade` then `_authorizeUpgrade` requires the exact implementation and `block.timestamp >= upgradeProposedAt + 48 hours`; `guardianCancelUpgrade` vetoes. Role grants through OZ AccessControl are instant, so an admin can revoke the guardian first. Both deploy scripts set admin = keeper = guardian = deployer. `MAX_REDEEM_TIMELOCK = 7 days` exceeds the 48 h upgrade window, and `redeem` is pausable, so users cannot be sure to exit inside the upgrade window (`A-custody-modules.md > 6. hypurrquant HyperVault structure`).
- `emergencyWithdrawToken` (ADMIN, only while paused) cannot touch USDC or the share token (`A-custody-modules.md > 6. hypurrquant HyperVault structure`).
- There is no permissionless exit and no in-kind exit: `redeem` needs a keeper `markRedeemReady` and is `whenNotPaused`; if the keeper disappears, funds on HyperCore are stuck until an admin acts (`D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 1. Headline answers`; `D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 7.3 Notable details`).
- Lockups: a per-request redeem timelock (1 h to 7 days) snapshotted at request; there is no deposit-side lockup (`D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 7.2 Lifecycle (V)`).
- Leader handover: nothing in either repository; changing a strategist root in BoringVault goes through the owner's timelock (48 h on vmUSD), too slow for a sale (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.1 Design comparison table`).
- The Hyperliquid parity list the research worked from: leader keeps at least 5%, 10% performance fee, leader trades but never withdraws depositor funds, 1-day default lockup the leader can extend, proportional position closing when free balance cannot cover a withdrawal, leader can close the vault to new deposits, full public visibility (`brief-part1-part2.md > Part 1: Our product and what is already decided`).

## 3. Platform mapping: what the research recommends we borrow and what to avoid

Custody core

- Keep custody small and non-upgradeable like BoringVault, but with no generic `manage`, no external mint, and no burn of another user's shares; `redeemInKind` lives in the custody contract. Expose one trading entry point, sketched as `execute(typedIntent)`, callable only by a single `executor` address stored in the vault (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.1 Design comparison table`; `A-custody-modules.md > 5.3 Recommendation for StrategyVault + Executor (I, design advice)`).
- Share supply integrity: no MINTER or BURNER role; minting only in the vault's own deposit function, burning only in its own exit functions; if deposit logic is ever replaceable, that change waits behind the long timelock (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.2 Recommended changes (ranked)`, change 3).
- A full RolesAuthority is not needed for one vault with one Executor; if a role table is used platform-wide, `redeemInKind` must not be `requiresAuth` and `setAuthority` must be timelocked in-vault (`A-custody-modules.md > 5.3 Recommendation for StrategyVault + Executor (I, design advice)`).
- Avoid `receive()` or handle native MON explicitly; stray native balances sit outside in-kind accounting. Only allowlisted tokens count toward valuation and in-kind payouts; the in-kind loop should iterate a held-asset list that can only shrink once a balance is zero (`A-custody-modules.md > 5.3 Recommendation for StrategyVault + Executor (I, design advice)`; `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.7 Risks (ranked)`, risks 12 and 13).
- Keep all value inside the vault: no limit orders, lending buffers or escrow contracts at launch; anything added later must be reachable by the in-kind path without the platform (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.2 Recommended changes (ranked)`, change 15).
- Clean-room implementation: no BoringVault source copied; patterns cited in design docs only (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.2 Recommended changes (ranked)`, change 17).

Executor

- Stay typed-only; do not add Merkle proofs. For USDC and WMON on one or two DEXes a Merkle layer would only duplicate what the typed intent already pins (router, selector, tokens, recipient, value) and add a root, leaf files and decoders to audit (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.3 Executor decision revisited`; `B-strategy-restrictions.md > 7.2 Hybrid or not?`).
- Borrow BoringSwapper internals: a per-venue adapter parses the router calldata and must match the typed intent (recipient, path endpoints, amount, zero native value); the Executor pulls exactly `amountIn` from the vault, approves exactly, calls, resets to zero, measures the realized output delta, checks it against the oracle with the 0.5% slippage bound (worst pair if several oracles), sends output to the vault and returns dust; the vault never approves a router (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.2 Recommended changes (ranked)`, change 6; `B-strategy-restrictions.md > 7.1 Limit-by-limit comparison`).
- The vault re-checks hard invariants after every Executor call: `totalSupply` unchanged; only `tokenIn` down and `tokenOut` up; no allowance left; post-trade USDC floor and 40% cap hold (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.2 Recommended changes (ranked)`, change 5).
- Rolling 20 trades per 24 h as a ring buffer of the last 20 trade timestamps: allow a trade only if the oldest slot is empty or older than 24 h, then overwrite it. Config or epoch changes must not reset it. Optionally add a per-direction USD-volume token bucket as a second brake (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.2 Recommended changes (ranked)`, change 7; `B-strategy-restrictions.md > 4.2 UManager.enforceRateLimit: not a rolling window, and actually broken`).
- Deadline: bound the intent's own deadline to `now + 120 s` and pass it to the router; a deadline computed onchain as `block.timestamp + x` is meaningless (`B-strategy-restrictions.md > 7.1 Limit-by-limit comparison`).
- Oracle wrapper per feed that enforces staleness and decimals once and is reused by the Executor, deposits, USDC exits and the breaker, plus a second-source deviation check; Monad test configs use 6 h staleness and ours must be much tighter (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.2 Recommended changes (ranked)`, change 14).
- Directional route table: the emergency risk role and handover mode use a reduce-only set (non-USDC to USDC only) held by a separate role, not a flag in the trading path. Add per-venue pause and a platform-wide adapter kill switch that never touches `redeemInKind` (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.2 Recommended changes (ranked)`, ranked table row 4 of section 2.1; `B-strategy-restrictions.md > 3.3 Mapping to our roles`; `B-strategy-restrictions.md > 7.1 Limit-by-limit comparison`).
- A later admin-only extension path for protocols that do not fit a typed swap could use a plain onchain mapping `(target, selector, argsHash) -> allowed` behind the timelock; more readable for depositors than a root (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.3 Executor decision revisited`).

Delays and roles

- Enforce every delay in our own code: Executor replacement, oracle changes, route-table additions, fee changes, lockup maximum and asset-list changes use propose/accept with an in-contract delay longer than the maximum lockup; no external timelock convention and no instant role grants that bypass it. The Executor address changes only via propose/accept; the emergency role may only set the Executor to none. Hard-code a `MAX_LOCKUP` constant so "longer than the maximum lockup" is a fixed number (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.2 Recommended changes (ranked)`, change 4; `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.1 Design comparison table`; `A-custody-modules.md > 5.3 Recommendation for StrategyVault + Executor (I, design advice)`).
- Pauser pattern (pause and unpause as separate keys, sender-scoped pause) is good for deposits and Executor trading and must never reach `redeemInKind` (`A-custody-modules.md > 5.3 Recommendation for StrategyVault + Executor (I, design advice)`).
- Emit old and new values on every config change (Executor, routes, oracles, fees, lockup, leader) and full structs on deposit and exit (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.2 Recommended changes (ranked)`, change 16; `B-strategy-restrictions.md > 5. Root updates: who, how, with what delay`).

Valuation, spread and reference-price band

- Keep per-transaction oracle valuation; do not publish a manager-pushed rate: the manager benefits from the price (fee and own stake), a pushed rate moves at most a band per update and is stale for a traded MON position, and its failure mode pauses everything (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.4 Valuation decision`; `C-accounting.md > 6.2 Should we publish a bounded exchange rate instead of live oracle valuation?`).
- Fail closed on any oracle or balance read in deposits and USDC exits; never count a failed read as zero (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.2 Recommended changes (ranked)`, change 11).
- Reference-price breaker: storage `refPrice` and `refTime`; after each successful deposit or USDC exit and via a permissionless `poke()`, move `refPrice` toward the live share price by at most `maxMoveBps * elapsed / window`; deposits and USDC exits (and the proportional sell path) revert when the live price is outside the band; no stored pause flag and no admin unpause; optionally price deposits at `max(live, refPrice)` and USDC exits at `min(live, refPrice)`; `redeemInKind` never reads any of it (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.2 Recommended changes (ranked)`, change 8; `C-accounting.md > 6.3 Recommended hybrid: oracle NAV plus an Accountant-style circuit breaker`).
- Spread: per asset (wider for WMON than USDC), and at least the oracle's deviation-update threshold because a push oracle can lag by up to that amount; the lockup stops same-block round trips (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.4 Valuation decision`; `C-accounting.md > 6.3 Recommended hybrid: oracle NAV plus an Accountant-style circuit breaker`; `D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 2.2 Share math and premium`).
- Virtual shares: hypurrquant's 1e6/1e6 is weak; use a larger share offset (the research suggests 10^3 to 10^6 extra share decimals) pending a donation-attack cost estimate for 6-decimal USDC and 18-decimal WMON; fee basis rounding should favor the user (`C-accounting.md > 4.3 Virtual offset and rounding (Verified)`; `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.1 Design comparison table`).

Deposits

- Add `minSharesOut` on deposit, an asset-denominated TVL cap for launch, and a `depositsOpen` flag separate from any pause, because both repositories couple "close" to "pause" and that blocks exits (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.2 Recommended changes (ranked)`, change 12; `D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 9.2 Close to new deposits`).
- Per-user caps are optional; if added, count locked principal and require `receiver == msg.sender` (`D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 9.1 Caps`).
- Port the permit fallback so a front-run permit does not DoS a deposit (`03-spike-plan.md > 2. Tests to port`).

Lockups

- Store each deposit's unlock time at deposit; extensions apply only to later deposits; hard `MAX_LOCKUP` constant; no third-party relock (require `receiver == msg.sender`, or lock per lot). A lockup fixed at deposit with a hard maximum is deterministic, so applying it to `redeemInKind` is not a discretionary pause; the research still asked for explicit sign-off (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.2 Recommended changes (ranked)`, change 10; `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.7 Risks (ranked)`, risk 6; `D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 3.4 Relevance to us`).
- The lockup check for in-kind must be a vault-internal timestamp, not an external hook; a reverting hook must never block redemption (`A-custody-modules.md > 5.3 Recommendation for StrategyVault + Executor (I, design advice)`).

Withdrawals and redeemInKind

- `redeemInKind` in the custody contract with no auth modifier, no pause check, no hook, no authority lookup, no oracle and no external module; it burns only `msg.sender`'s shares (or an approved owner's) and pays `balance * shares / totalSupply` of each held token (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.2 Recommended changes (ranked)`, change 2).
- `redeemInKind` must survive a failing token: a `skipTokens` list (caller forfeits those tokens to remaining holders) or a pull-based claim credit; one token's `transfer` revert must never revert the whole exit. Motivated by Circle USDC blacklist or pause on Monad (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.2 Recommended changes (ranked)`, change 1).
- Operators or relayers may trigger exits but proceeds always go to the share owner (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.2 Recommended changes (ranked)`, change 13).
- No withdrawal queue at launch; the USDC path pays from the floor or sells pro rata with oracle and user minimums and reverts cleanly on a thin pool. Add an ERC-7540 async redeem alongside (never instead of) `redeemInKind` only when positions cannot be split in kind, pro-rata sales routinely exceed the slippage bound, or perps and leverage are added. If added: payout fixed at request or `min(rate at request, rate now)`, burn or exclude shares when payout is fixed, reserve funds at readiness, user cancel never pausable, receiver = controller, per-user request cap (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.5 Queue decision`; `D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 8.2 Do we need a queue at launch? Recommendation: No.`).
- Do not import deny lists, remediation, refunds, withdraw capacity or any exit throttle (`D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 11. Decision comparison summary`).

Performance fee with high-water mark (later phase)

- Per-user basis in share-price terms: on each deposit update the user's weighted average entry share price `P_entry`; migrate it on share transfers, rounding in the user's favor. Fee at exit paid in shares: `feeShares = shares * max(0, P - P_entry) / P * feeBps / 1e4`, minted or transferred to the leader before the exit burns the rest; NAV unchanged, no fee liability in the vault, fee shares help the leader stay above 5%. This is a per-user high-water mark set at entry. USDC exits use the live price already computed in the transaction; `redeemInKind` uses the last stored `refPrice` (a storage read that cannot fail or be paused), never an oracle. Fee changes timelocked longer than the maximum lockup, applied only to later deposits (hypurrquant snapshot), with a hard-coded maximum near the 10% target (for example 20%). No HWM reset. Any management fee uses `min(shares now, shares at last accrual) * min(old price, new price)`, time-prorated, in minted shares; any fee held as assets is excluded from NAV. Decide loss carry-forward explicitly (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.6 Fees`; `C-accounting.md > 5. Per-user vs global high-water mark (for the future 10% fee)`).

Leader stake and handover

- Leader stake: after any deposit by someone else require `leaderShares * 1e4 >= 500 * totalSupply` (reject or cap the deposit); block leader exits that would drop below 5% while other depositors remain; never block others' exits on this ratio; track leader shares as non-transferable or separately (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.2 Recommended changes (ranked)`, change 9; `D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 9.5 Leader stake enforcement`).
- Handover on agent sale: switch the route table to reduce-only in the same transaction as the sale, bump the ownership epoch so old session-key intents revert, and delay the new owner's first trade by at least the maximum lockup; depositors can use every exit throughout (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.1 Design comparison table`; `03-spike-plan.md > 1. Spike steps`, S13).

Deployment

- Deployment scripts should assert the final onchain state (roles, delays, oracles, routes, fees, caps) against the intended config, because Veda's Monad repo config does not match its live deployment (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.7 Risks (ranked)`, risk 16).

## 6. Gaps and weak spots

- No MON/USD oracle was identified. Neither repository references a MON feed; Veda's Monad swapper script wires only USDC and mUSD Chainlink feeds. The spread, the breaker band, the freshness limit and the deviation check all depend on the feed's heartbeat and deviation threshold, which are unknown (`03-spike-plan.md > 3. Open questions`, 1; `C-accounting.md > 7. Open questions`, 5). The register's "Oracles: unresolved" is the single largest dependency for the vault.
- Whether a Uniswap TWAP on Monad is deep enough to serve as the second oracle source for the 2% deviation check is unknown (`03-spike-plan.md > 3. Open questions`, 2).
- The leader stake, handover, and stake-on-sale have no precedent in either repository and must be designed from scratch; the research's proposal blocks leader exits, which the register's "never on exits" wording does not anticipate (`D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 9.5 Leader stake enforcement`; `02-platform-mapping.md > 2.7 Risks (ranked)`, risks 9 and 10).
- Lockup interactions look unsound as currently registered: "later of" on top-ups and on transfers combined with open third-party deposits gives anyone a way to extend a victim's lock (`D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 3.4 Relevance to us`; the transfer case is Inference). Also, a depositor has no exit at all during the first 24 h; the research calls this a deliberate trade-off that must be explained (`D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 12. Open questions`, 3).
- The breaker's band and catch-up speed need historical MON volatility data; too tight blocks honest users, too loose lets oracle lag through (`C-accounting.md > 7. Open questions`, 6). During a real crash the breaker can block USDC exits until `refPrice` catches up, pushing everyone into in-kind exits (`C-accounting.md > 6.3 Recommended hybrid`).
- The fee design requires per-user entry prices to exist from launch and a stored `refPrice` for in-kind pricing; whether in-kind exits pay a fee at all, and loss carry-forward, are undecided (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.6 Fees`; `C-accounting.md > 7. Open questions`, 7 and 8).
- Dropping an asset from the allowlist while the vault holds it would strand depositors' claim unless the in-kind loop uses a separate held-asset list (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.7 Risks (ranked)`, risk 13).
- Thin Monad liquidity: the revert rate of the USDC sell path relative to pool depth is unknown; the research recommends a TVL cap and monitoring, with queue triggers defined but no data (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.5 Queue decision`; `03-spike-plan.md > 3. Open questions`, 15).
- USDC on Monad: whether Circle's token includes blacklist and pause functions and who controls them was not inspected; the skip or credit design assumes it does (`03-spike-plan.md > 3. Open questions`, 12).
- Anvil forking of Monad may not reproduce gas, reserve balance rules or sub-second block timestamps; many blocks can share a timestamp, which matters for any per-second logic (`03-spike-plan.md > 3. Open questions`, 11; `B-strategy-restrictions.md > 9. Open questions`, 9).
- No tests were run anywhere; every claim about BoringVault and hypurrquant behavior comes from reading code (`01-codebase-report.md > 1. Versions, licenses, audit status, summaries`). Certora specs for BoringSwapper were not read and may hold invariants worth mirroring (`03-spike-plan.md > 3. Open questions`, 16).
- Licensing: BoringVault is SEL-1.0. The plan says "borrowing patterns"; the research says clean room with no derivative code (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.2 Recommended changes (ranked)`, change 17). Nothing in this research covered the license of the Morpho code the register also names as a pattern source.
- Venue: the research covers Uniswap V3 and V4 adapters and Monad addresses only; Kuru is not mentioned anywhere in the sources.
- Documentation drift is a live risk: Veda's Monad repo config disagrees with the chain on timelock and fees, so our own deployment needs a state-assertion gate (`A-custody-modules.md > 2.5 Live Monad vmUSD wiring (read onchain, V)`; `C-accounting.md > 3.1 Yield streaming`).
- The register's 20% "pause" and its unpause path are undefined; in both repositories "pause" meant no exits, and unpausing is a risk-adding action the emergency role must not hold (`A-custody-modules.md > 2.6 Pauser`; `C-accounting.md > 2.4 What pause affects, and who can unpause`).

## 7. Open questions needing a human decision

1. Skip-list forfeit or pull-based credit when a token transfer reverts in `redeemInKind`? Recommended: pull-based credit, with credited amounts excluded from the vault's counted balance for valuation and other users' in-kind math. Reason: forfeiting a blacklisted user's USDC to other holders is a designed loss of user funds; a credit preserves the claim (`03-spike-plan.md > 3. Open questions`, 4; the exclusion rule is Inference modeled on hypurrquant `reservedEvmBalance`, `D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 7.3 Notable details`).
2. Is the 5% leader stake in launch scope, and if so reject or cap diluting deposits? Recommended: in scope, reject with a clear error rather than partial fill, and scope the exit-side check to the leader only. Reason: partial fills complicate `minSharesOut` and UI; the register's "never on exits" stays true for depositors (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.2 Recommended changes (ranked)`, change 9).
3. Does the leader stake move with the agent NFT on sale, or must the new owner post it? Recommended: the new owner posts it before handover ends; the seller's shares become ordinary depositor shares under the lockup. Reason (Inference): shares are held by the owner's wallet, not by the agent's token-bound account, which holds only skills and identity (`brief-part1-part2.md > Part 1`; `03-spike-plan.md > 3. Open questions`, 5).
4. Should third-party deposits to a receiver be allowed? Recommended: no, require `receiver == msg.sender` at launch. Reason: removes lock griefing outright (`D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 12. Open questions`, 5).
5. Should locked shares be transferable with lock propagation (register) or blocked while locked (BoringVault)? Recommended: block transfers of locked lots, or apply the receiver's "later of" only to the transferred lot. Reason (Inference): whole-balance propagation is a griefing vector.
6. Virtual share offset size for 6-decimal USDC and 18-decimal WMON? Recommended: choose after V-24; the research suggests more than hypurrquant's 1e6/1e6. Reason: negligible cost (`03-spike-plan.md > 3. Open questions`, 14).
7. Should in-kind exits pay the performance fee, and at which price? Recommended: yes, in shares at the stored `refPrice`. Reason: fee-free in-kind invites fee avoidance and the stored price keeps in-kind oracle-free (`C-accounting.md > 7. Open questions`, 8).
8. Who keeps surplus when the USDC sell path beats the oracle minimum? Recommended: the withdrawing user. Reason: the user sold their own pro-rata share (`D-deposits-withdrawals.md > 12. Open questions`, 10).
9. Executor as a separate contract or inside the vault? Recommended: separate, with vault-side post-trade invariants. Reason: keeps the immutable custody contract small and lets the Executor be replaced behind the timelock (`A-custody-modules.md > 8. Open questions`, 6).
10. Venue for launch: Uniswap V4, Uniswap V3, or Kuru? Recommended: a Uniswap venue. Reason: the research has adapter grammar, Monad addresses and a live Veda swapper on Uniswap; Kuru has no research coverage (`03-spike-plan.md > Setup`).
11. Loss carry-forward for the performance fee? Recommended: none across a full exit and re-entry (hypurrquant model); the weighted entry price already carries losses within a position. Reason: simplest state that still matches per-user fairness (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.6 Fees`; `03-spike-plan.md > 3. Open questions`, 13).
12. Band and catch-up speed for the reference-price breaker, and the per-asset spread sizes? Recommended: derive from the chosen MON feed's deviation threshold and from S8's blocked-operations count; spread at least the deviation threshold. Reason: no code can answer it (`C-accounting.md > 7. Open questions`, 5 and 6).
13. Who unpauses after the 20% circuit-breaker pause, and does that action pass through the timelock? Recommended: the owner or platform admin, never the emergency role, and the pause must never touch any exit. Reason: unpausing adds risk (`A-custody-modules.md > 2.6 Pauser`).
14. Held-asset list versus allowlist: when a feed is dropped, does the asset stay in in-kind payouts? Recommended: yes, always. Reason: dropping it strands depositors' claims (`02-platform-mapping.md > 2.7 Risks (ranked)`, risk 13).
